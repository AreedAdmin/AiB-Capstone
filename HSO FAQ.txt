Question: What are some monetisation strategies Kaluza is supportive of to avoid curtailment effectively?
Answer: In general, we would like to hear your opinions (backed with data) as our consultants, and this is one of the key deliverables we are after. But, let me share some notes from our internal meetings that summarise our position:
1. Revenue Potential from Avoided Curtailment
Increased utilisation of renewable energy: By utilising power that would otherwise be curtailed, Kaluza could claim the wholesale value of that energy. For Orkney, this translates to a valuable opportunity to support the local grid, lower emissions, and improve the efficiency of renewable energy use.
Revenue-sharing agreements: If Kaluza can negotiate agreements with wind turbine operators, it could share in the avoided curtailment value, ensuring a revenue stream that benefits both Kaluza and the generators.

2. Consumer Pricing Models for Curtailment Benefits
Discounted energy pricing for households: By offering lower-cost energy derived from avoided curtailment, Kaluza can attract more households to the DR scheme. If the energy is significantly cheaper than grid rates, consumer participation will likely increase, creating a robust revenue source.
Subscription-based models: Implementing a subscription fee for access to curtailed energy, or a tiered pricing model based on usage, would generate predictable revenue from households, offsetting the costs of DR implementation.

3. Government and Regulatory Support
Subsidies and incentives: Governments often provide subsidies to support renewable energy efficiency and curtailment reduction initiatives. If Kaluza can secure such incentives, the cost of managing avoided curtailment can be reduced, making the revenue more impactful.
Environmental credits: Avoided curtailment may qualify for environmental credits or certificates, which can be sold to companies seeking to offset their carbon footprint, adding an additional revenue stream.

4. Infrastructure and Operational Costs
Cost-effectiveness of DR infrastructure: To monetise avoided curtailment effectively, the cost of deploying and maintaining DR technology (such as smart heaters and control hubs) must be lower than the value generated from reduced curtailment.
Operational efficiency: As a DR solution provider, Kaluza’s revenue would also depend on the efficiency of its operations. Efficient systems for activating and deactivating devices, with minimal downtime, help ensure that avoided curtailment remains a viable revenue source.

5. Market Demand and Consumer Adoption
Demand for affordable, renewable energy: High local interest in renewable energy at reduced costs would support Kaluza’s pricing model for avoided curtailment. Ensuring competitive pricing and transparency about savings will likely improve adoption.
Scalability of DR adoption: The revenue from avoided curtailment increases with DR penetration levels. High DR penetration allows Kaluza to capture more of the curtailed energy, improving revenue potential from avoided curtailment in proportion to system demand.

6. Long-Term Contracts with Wind Generators
Predictability of avoided curtailment revenue: Long-term contracts with wind farms could provide a stable revenue stream by allowing Kaluza to monetise avoided curtailment consistently. This stability is crucial in assessing whether the approach is sustainable.
