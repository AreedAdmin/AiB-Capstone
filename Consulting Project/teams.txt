Background to Company and Project
AstraZeneca is a global, science-led biopharmaceutical company focused on the discovery,
development, and commercialisation of innovative prescription medicines across oncology,
rare diseases, and bio-pharmaceuticals. With a strong global presence and a rapidly expanding
footprint in China, the company is investing significantly to enhance its R&D and manufacturing
capabilities, particularly in advanced modalities such as cell therapy. China represents a
strategic growth market, offering a combination of scientific innovation, manufacturing
excellence, and a rapidly evolving healthcare ecosystem. As part of its long-term expansion,
AstraZeneca is seeking to strengthen its local supplier base to support the development and
delivery of next-generation therapies. This project will focus on supporting AstraZeneca’s
procurement and supply chain strategy in China by identifying optimal approaches to supplier
base establishment. The aim is to better understand the local market landscape, assess
potential partners, and define a robust, scalable supplier strategy aligned with the company’s
growth ambitions in the region.
Project Briefing
This project aims to address the following key question:
How should AstraZeneca establish / optimise a high-quality, resilient, and scalable supplier
base in China to support its expanding portfolio of cell therapy?
As AstraZeneca continues to invest in China, the development of a strong local supplier
ecosystem is critical to ensuring operational efficiency, regulatory compliance, and long-term
sustainability. The student team will be expected to conduct a comprehensive market analysis
of the Chinese supplier landscape, focusing on key categories relevant to advanced therapy
manufacturing (e.g., raw materials, specialised equipment, logistics, and contract
manufacturing). The project should evaluate potential suppliers based on criteria such as
capability, quality standards, regulatory alignment, scalability, cost competitiveness, and
strategic fit. In addition, students should assess the risks and challenges associated with
supplier selection in China, including geopolitical considerations, supply chain resilience,
intellectual property concerns, and regulatory complexity. The team is encouraged to
benchmark best practices from both global pharmaceutical companies and leading local
players operating in China. Insights into how competitors structure and manage their supplier
networks will be valuable in informing recommendations. Expected outcomes of the project
include clear, actionable guidance on how AstraZeneca can establish and develop its supplier
base in China. This should include recommendations on supplier selection frameworks,
partnership models (e.g., strategic partnerships vs transactional relationships), and potential
approaches to supplier development and risk mitigation. The primary deliverable will be a final
presentation summarising key findings, insights, and recommendations, supported by detailed
analytical appendices. Additional useful outputs may include a supplier evaluation framework,
a shortlist of potential suppliers or partner profiles, and a structured market map of the Chinese
ecosystem. Where feasible, the project would benefit from primary research, including
interviews with potential future customers, industry experts, or stakeholders within the Chinese
healthcare and biotech ecosystem. These insights would help validate findings and strengthen
the practical relevance of the recommendations. Overall, the project should provide
AstraZeneca with a robust, data-driven foundation to inform its supplier strategy in China and
support its long-term growth and innovation objectives in the region.
Skills needed for the Project
Analytical, Financial, Research, Computational. Objectives for the project; Goal 1: Complete a
comprehensive market analysis identifying and categorizing at least 50 potential suppliers
across 5 key categories (raw materials, specialized equipment, logistics, contract
manufacturing, and ancillary services) relevant to advanced therapy manufacturing in China,
with detailed profiles including capability assessments, certifications, and market positioning.

weighted criteria (capability, quality standards, regulatory alignment, scalability, cost
competitiveness, strategic fit, IP protection, and supply chain resilience), test it against 10-15

AstraZeneca teams. Goal 3: Conduct and document at least 12-15 primary research interviews
with key stakeholders including potential suppliers (5-6), industry experts (3-4), healthcare

Deliver a comprehensive final presentation and supporting documentation including: (1)
actionable supplier selection strategy with 3-5 prioritized recommendations, (2) shortlist of 8-12
qualified suppliers with partnership model recommendations, (3) risk mitigation framework
addressing geopolitical, IP, and regulatory challenges, and (4) implementation roadmap with
clear next steps for AstraZeneca's China supplier development program.

Background to Company and Project
CeramTec is a global leader in advanced ceramics, serving high‑precision industrial and
medical markets. In recent years, increasing market volatility, rising cost pressure and
heterogeneous customer behavior have made static pricing models less effective. To prepare
for a future Dynamic Pricing system, CeramTec aims to understand which pricing drivers can be
reliably derived from existing data.
The company currently holds extensive information across SAP (orders, production, cost data),
Salesforce (quotes, wins/losses), production/SIOP (capacity and utilization), and external
market indicators. However, data quality, historical completeness and structural consistency
vary across systems. To avoid premature system implementation and focus on analytical
maturity, CeramTec is initiating a 6‑week student project to evaluate feasibility based solely on
static data extracts.
The project represents an opportunity to explore (1) whether pricing‑relevant patterns can be
extracted from historical data, (2) how strongly internal and external drivers influence margins,
and (3) which data improvements are needed before deploying a full Dynamic Pricing solution.
The outcome will directly inform CeramTec’s multi‑year pricing roadmap and ensure that future
investments in automation and AI‑based pricing are grounded in evidence‑based insights.
Project Briefing
The project focuses on answering a central question: to what extent can meaningful
pricing‑relevant drivers be extracted from CeramTec’s existing historical data, and what level of
analytical maturity is necessary before the company can adopt a Dynamic Pricing system. The
students will work exclusively with static data dumps and are expected to determine whether
key drivers—such as Win Probability, Plant Utilization, customer and offer segmentation, or
external market indicators—can be reliably computed, interpreted, and validated. The intention
is not to develop production‑ready models, but to create a fact‑based feasibility assessment
that clearly indicates which drivers are ready for future implementation, which would require
further data investment, and which are currently not viable.
The project is designed to generate three major outcomes for the business. First, it will create
foundational clarity for the Dynamic Pricing roadmap by identifying which analytical elements
can realistically be operationalised in the short to medium term. Second, it will increase
transparency around data quality and structural limitations within the company’s Salesforce,
SAP, production/SIOP, and external datasets, helping CeramTec understand the extent of its
data gaps and inconsistencies. Third, the project will provide prototype insights—such as an
initial Win Probability model, clustering-based segmentation results, and preliminary utilization
KPIs—that can guide management in evaluating model design options, potential data pipelines,
and future investments in automation or forecasting solutions.
Students will deliver a final presentation supported by detailed analytical appendices and a
concise management summary. Throughout the project, they will also produce several
specialised outputs, including a data inventory and quality assessment covering history,
structure, and completeness; a feature catalog documenting variables, transformations, and
engineering logic; baseline and advanced Win Probability prototypes supported by performance
metrics, lift charts, and calibration plots; interpretable clustering and segmentation results;
derived Plant Utilization KPIs together with an analysis of their stability and pricing relevance;
and a shortlist of external market indicators, such as foreign exchange trends or commodity
indices, accompanied by evidence of their potential influence. All results will be consolidated
into a comprehensive Dynamic Pricing Feasibility Report that summarises analytical findings,
data risks, technical prerequisites, and recommended next steps.
Skills needed for the Project
The project team should have strong analytical skills and the ability to perform exploratory data
analysis to identify patterns, data gaps, and quality issues. Team members should possess solid
computational and machine‑learning capabilities, including experience with Python and
libraries such as pandas, scikit‑learn, NumPy, and visualization tools. They should be
comfortable building and validating classification models, handling class imbalance, and
engineering meaningful features from heterogeneous datasets.

Background to Your Company and Project
Our company operates in a highly customer‑centric environment where strategic decisions,
opportunity qualification, and forecasting accuracy strongly depend on the quality of
information captured in Salesforce. While structured CRM fields provide a solid foundation for
reporting, a significant share of customer knowledge resides in unstructured data such as
meeting notes, emails, call summaries, and internal comments. These rich insights often
remain underutilized because they are difficult to search, interpret, and aggregate at scale.
The need for this project arises from two converging opportunities:
(1) Unlocking hidden qualitative signals that can strengthen sales decision‑making, and
(2) Leveraging recent advances in agentic AI and large language models to process unstructured
data autonomously.
The proposed student project explores how modern AI agents can extract meaningful customer
signals—such as sentiment, intent, risks, objections, and buying readiness—and combine them
with existing Salesforce opportunity data. This will enable a more holistic and data‑driven Win
Probability estimation and deliver actionable Next Best Action recommendations for sales
teams.
Project Briefing
Our business relies heavily on Salesforce as the central system of record for customer
interactions, yet a significant portion of valuable information—emails, call summaries, internal
comments, meeting notes, and activity logs—exists in unstructured form. Traditional analytics
mainly leverage structured CRM fields, leaving qualitative signals such as sentiment, intent,
objections, risks, or competitive mentions largely unused. This creates an opportunity to
strengthen decision‑making, improve forecasting accuracy, and enable more consistent sales
execution.
The student project aims to explore how modern agentic AI can unlock these hidden insights.
The core challenge is to design and prototype an autonomous multi‑step AI agent capable of
ingesting unstructured Salesforce data, extracting meaningful customer signals, and
synthesizing them into actionable outputs. This includes topic identification, sentiment and
tone analysis, risk or blocker detection, customer intent recognition, and general insight mining.
Each extracted signal should be accompanied by a confidence score to support transparency
and user trust.
To translate insights into business value, the project should merge these qualitative signals with
structured opportunity attributes in Salesforce, enabling an enriched Win Probability model.
This hybrid approach will help sales teams better assess pipeline health and identify high‑risk
deals earlier. In addition, the AI system should generate automated Next Best Action
recommendations informed by the detected patterns and confidence levels, helping sales reps
respond faster and more consistently to customer needs.
Expected Outcomes for the Business
Improved opportunity forecasting by integrating qualitative factors alongside traditional
structured data.
Stronger sales execution with AI‑driven recommendations tailored to customer context.
Enhanced decision‑support through automated surfacing of sentiment, risks, objections, or
buying readiness signals.
Foundational architecture for future agentic AI use cases in CRM and customer‑facing
processes.
Demonstration of feasibility for scalable, autonomous text‑based insight mining within
enterprise systems.
Most Useful Deliverables
A presentation with detailed appendices outlining approach, findings, and implications.
A working agentic AI prototype capable of end‑to‑end insight extraction from unstructured
Salesforce records.
A signal extraction taxonomy and classification framework (topics, sentiment, objections, risks,
intent, etc.).
A confidence scoring methodology with explanation of its logic and reliability limits.
A Win Probability prototype model combining structured and unstructured data features.
A Recommendation Engine for automated Next Best Actions.
A Feasibility & Implementation Report covering data readiness, technical architecture, risks,
scalability, and ROI potential.
Skills needed for the Project
The project team should have strong technical and analytical capabilities, particularly in Python
programming and modern natural‑language‑processing methods. Students will need
experience working with large language models, including prompting techniques and multi‑step
agentic workflows, as well as a solid understanding of machine‑learning concepts such as text
classification, embeddings, and feature engineering. They should be comfortable handling
semi‑structured and unstructured data formats like Salesforce notes, emails, and activity logs.
In addition, familiarity with CRM systems—ideally Salesforce—is beneficial, especially an
understanding of opportunity stages, customer interactions, and typical sales processes. The
ability to translate qualitative text signals into meaningful business insights will be essential for
constructing an enriched Win Probability model. Strong analytical and problem‑solving skills
are required to break down complex workflows, evaluate model outputs critically, and iterate
toward improved solutions. Clear communication skills are also important, as students will
need to document their methods, present their findings to non‑technical stakeholders, and
conduct brief interviews with sales team members to validate insights. Finally, a creative
mindset and willingness to experiment with new AI techniques will help the team design
effective signal categories, agent tasks, and recommendation logic.

SynX Therapeutics is building a programmable discovery platform for peptide-like therapeutics
(“SynPeps”) using engineered translation systems combined with AI-enabled optimisation. The
platform is designed to enable access to non-canonical chemistry and to address historical
limitations of peptides, including stability, permeability, and immunogenicity.
The company has raised $2.35 million from a group of leading technology entrepreneurs,
venture capital investors, and life science investors. This capital has supported initial platform
development and early validation work.
As SynX prepares for its Seed round, a central strategic decision must now be made: which
therapeutic indications should anchor the company’s initial clinical programmes?
This decision will shape capital allocation, development sequencing, partnering discussions,
and the overall investor narrative.
Project Briefing
SynX Therapeutics is preparing for its next fundraising round and must define a clear and
defensible clinical entry strategy. The company has developed a programmable peptide
discovery platform and has identified a defined set of 8 therapeutic indications where the
technology may offer meaningful differentiation. These candidate indications differ
substantially in biological rationale, development timelines, regulatory complexity, competitive
intensity, and capital requirements. A structured prioritisation is required to determine which
programmes should anchor the company’s next phase of development.
The project will address the following question: which indications should SynX prioritise to
achieve (i) a capital-efficient first-in-human proof point and (ii) long-term platform value
creation? The company is considering a dual-track approach. The first track is intended to
deliver early clinical validation and reduce perceived platform risk through a focused and
efficient development pathway. The second track is intended to maximise strategic and
economic upside by selecting an indication that demonstrates the broader potential of the
platform. The student team will evaluate the shortlisted indications and recommend one
candidate for each track.
The analysis should be comparative and evidence-based. The team is expected to design a
structured evaluation framework that captures platform fit, clinical and regulatory pathway
considerations, economic attractiveness, competitive landscape, and strategic positioning. All
assumptions should be explicit and consistently applied across indications. High-level
quantitative modelling should be included, covering total and serviceable addressable market
estimates, indicative development timelines to Phase II proof-of-concept, and approximate
capital requirements to key inflection points. Sensitivity analysis should be applied to the most
material assumptions to test the robustness of conclusions.
In addition to desk research and modelling, the team is encouraged to conduct a limited
number of targeted interviews with relevant stakeholders, such as clinical key opinion leaders
or former pharmaceutical business development professionals. The purpose of these
interviews is to validate core assumptions regarding clinical feasibility, adoption dynamics,
competitive differentiation, and partnering attractiveness. Interviews should be structured and
hypothesis-driven, with insights integrated into the comparative analysis.
The expected output is an investor-ready presentation of approximately 15–20 slides, supported
by detailed analytical appendices. The presentation should clearly describe the evaluation
framework, summarise the comparative analysis, outline the capital and timeline implications
of the recommended programmes, and articulate the principal risks and trade-offs. The
recommendation should be logically argued and directly usable in fundraising discussions.
Supporting materials should include a transparent quantitative model with documented
assumptions and sources.
Skills needed for the Project
The project requires a team with strong analytical and quantitative capability, including the
ability to build and stress-test market models (e.g., TAM/SAM), estimate development timelines
and capital requirements at a high level, and perform sensitivity analysis using Excel or
equivalent tools. The team should be comfortable conducting structured competitive and
industry analysis and translating complex inputs into clear, defensible strategic
recommendations. Familiarity with pharmaceutical development pathways, regulatory
considerations, and basic biotech economics is preferred, although deep scientific expertise is
not required. In addition, the team should be able to design and conduct focused stakeholder or
KOL interviews and synthesise qualitative insights into the broader analytical framework. The
final output should reflect clear reasoning, disciplined assumptions, and concise
communication suitable for an investor-facing context.

Background to Company and Project
The Design Museum is a leading cultural institution in London, attracting a diverse audience
through a dynamic programme of temporary exhibitions that explore contemporary design,
innovation, and creativity. These exhibitions vary significantly in theme, scale, and public
appeal, making them a core driver of visitor engagement, revenue generation, and brand
visibility.
In recent years, the museum has experienced increasing variability in visitor numbers across
different exhibitions. This fluctuation is influenced by multiple factors - seasonality, marketing
effectiveness, competing cultural events, public holidays, weather patterns, and the inherent
popularity of specific exhibition topics. As the museum continues to expand its programming
and operate in a competitive cultural landscape, the ability to accurately forecast visitor
attendance has become strategically important.
Operational Planning Complexity
Unpredictable visitor numbers make it difficult to plan staffing, manage capacity, and optimise
front‑of‑house operations. A forecasting model would support smoother operational
decision‑making.
Revenue and Resource Optimisation
Temporary exhibitions drive ticket sales, retail performance, café demand, and membership
conversions. More accurate forecasts would enable better budgeting and resource allocation.
Strategic Exhibition Planning
Understanding the drivers of visitor behaviour would help the museum evaluate exhibition
concepts, anticipate demand, and refine programming strategy.
Data‑Driven Decision Making
The museum holds valuable historical data but lacks a structured, analytical approach to
converting it into actionable insights. This project presents an opportunity to build internal
capability.
External Factors and Market Dynamics
London’s cultural sector is highly competitive, with many institutions hosting major exhibitions
simultaneously. A forecasting model would help the museum anticipate the impact of external
events and trends.
Opportunity for Innovation
With advances in analytics and machine learning, the museum can adopt modern forecasting
techniques that go beyond traditional intuition‑based planning.
This project therefore represents a timely opportunity to transform how the Design Museum
predicts demand, enabling more confident decision‑making and supporting long‑term
organisational resilience.
Project Briefing
Forecasting Temporary Exhibition Visitors at the Design Museum
The Design Museum is seeking support in developing a robust, data‑driven forecasting model to
predict visitor numbers for future temporary exhibitions. Temporary exhibitions are central to
the museum’s public offer and financial sustainability, yet attendance varies significantly
depending on theme, timing, marketing activity, and external factors. This variability makes it
challenging to plan staffing, manage capacity, forecast revenue, and optimise operational
resources.
Core Question for Students
How can the Design Museum accurately forecast visitor numbers for upcoming temporary
exhibitions using historical data, exhibition characteristics, and relevant external variables?
The project should explore which factors most strongly influence attendance and develop a
predictive model (or suite of models) that the museum can use to anticipate demand under
different scenarios. Students are encouraged to test multiple analytical approaches, justify
their assumptions, and provide evidence‑based recommendations, in line with the
programme’s emphasis on “well‑reasoned, evidence‑based conclusions.”
Skills needed for the Project
To deliver this project effectively, students will need a blend of analytical and interpersonal
skills that reflect the Design Museum’s values of curiosity, impact, empathy, and collaboration.
Analytical & Technical Skills: Strong quantitative analysis and problem‑solving abilities.
Experience with forecasting or statistical modelling using tools such as Python, R, or Excel.
Ability to work with and interpret historical datasets. Strategic & Business Insight: Capacity to
translate analytical findings into practical recommendations that create real organisational
impact. Understanding of how forecasting supports operational planning and visitor
experience. Communication & Collaboration: Clear communication skills to present insights to
non‑technical stakeholders. A collaborative mindset, working closely with museum teams and
showing empathy for operational realities. Mindset & Approach: Curiosity to explore patterns,
test assumptions, and investigate what drives visitor behaviour. Willingness to iterate, refine,
and co‑create solutions with museum staff.

Background to Company and Project
TxODDS is a B2B sports betting data company with 25 years of industry experience, operating
from offices in London, Chicago, and Belgrade with over 250 employees globally. Our mission is
to be the most trusted data infrastructure in the sports pricing ecosystem. We provide betting
operators, trading firms, market makers, and professional bettors with the foundation to price
with confidence, driven by industry-leading speed, accuracy, and reliability. Our clients include
leading global betting operators such as bet365, William Hill, DraftKings, and Hong Kong Jockey
Club and etc.
TxODDS focuses on five core verticals: Ultra-low latency aggregated odds, scout-collected live
scores, pricing management solutions, historical data, and TxLine—a blockchain-based data
distribution platform built on Solana.
The development of TxLine is driven by both opportunity and industry gaps. No sports data
provider has yet successfully leveraged blockchain for data distribution. TxLine introduces a
modular, unbundled sales model, allowing customers to purchase only the data they need,
catering to varying budgets and use cases. Additionally, token-based payments reduce
contractual friction and accelerate onboarding.This project aims to validate the commercial
viability, scalability, and market adoption of this innovative model.
Project Briefing
This project offers students from Imperial College Business School the opportunity to
collaborate directly with the TxLine project team at TxODDS London office, exploring and
validating the commercial viability, scalability, and market adoption of this innovative
blockchain-based data distribution model.
Students will conduct structured market research, including interviews with potential customer
groups, to understand different customer types, their specific data needs, and their willingness
to adopt modular, token-based purchasing models. Based on these insights, students are
expected to propose feasible sales strategies, product bundles, and marketing channels.
Findings will be compiled into a comprehensive PowerPoint presentation with detailed
appendices and delivered to the TxODDS steering committee and TxLine team, followed by a
Q&A session.
Unlike global sports data industry leaders such as Sportradar and Genius Sports, which
emphasize product breadth, TxODDS focuses on the core value of sports betting data
infrastructure: time-sensitive probability shifts with financial consequences. In other words, our
value lies in enabling clients to act on opportunities and make profitable decisions in markets
where outcomes change by the second. Leveraging this expertise, we provide ultra-fast
aggregated odds and live scores collected by scouts, delivering unmatched speed, accuracy,
and competitive edge.
The full opening of sports betting in North America, the growth of prediction markets, and the
rapid global adoption of blockchain technologies present a unique opportunity to reshape
traditional sports betting data delivery and unlock innovative commercial models.
Viewing sports betting as a time-sensitive, financially outcome-driven market also highlights
potential adjacent market opportunities. The project encourages students to explore TxLine’s
applications in related domains, such as financial trading or other real-time analytics sectors,
where speed, reliability, and competitive advantage are equally critical.
